# Operating Method — Consulting Deliverable Playbook
**Origin:** distilled from the much. Consulting / MDR Devices interview case-study project (same repo, `claude/odoo-sol-project-setup-u77k8z` branch). Client-agnostic — this file carries the *discipline*, not that project's content.

---

## 1. Governing distinction: pitch/plan-stage vs. execution-stage

Never fabricate anything that implies a decision has already been made by someone other than you:
- No invented signatures, named counterparties' commitments, or executed agreements.
- No fake dates, fee figures, or numbers presented as final when they're actually placeholders — mark placeholders as placeholders, visibly.
- Every "we will" in a plan must trace to something demonstrated, verified, or explicitly scoped as a to-be-confirmed item — never asserted as already true.

## 2. Verify, don't assume

- Every factual claim (a number, a date, a regulatory citation, a competitor's stated position) gets checked before it's used, not pattern-matched from what sounds plausible.
- When live-system access exists, claims about what the system does are proven by actually driving the system and capturing the real result — not described from memory or inference.
- Unverified claims — including about yourself (experience, track record) — get the same scrutiny as claims about a third party. If it isn't confirmed, don't assert it as a fact; caveat it or leave it out.

## 3. Audit in rounds, adversarially

- Treat a first draft as a first draft. Run explicit review passes against your own output before calling it done:
  - **Fact/consistency sweep** — do all numbers, names, and dates agree everywhere they appear across every document?
  - **Audience-leak sweep** — has any internal/prep-only language leaked into an external-facing document?
  - **Convention sweep** — one spelling/terminology convention enforced throughout, not mixed.
  - **Adversarial simulation** — actually role-play the toughest questions a skeptical reader/counterparty would ask, and check the material survives them.
- Log what each round found and fixed — the audit trail itself is evidence of rigor, not overhead to skip.

## 4. Separate audiences into separate artifacts

- Keep the polished, external-facing package distinct from the personal working/prep material (raw research, talking points, rehearsal notes). Never let one leak into the other.
- External-facing documents carry consistent authorship/branding; internal prep material is clearly marked as never going to the counterparty.

## 5. Plan before big build bursts, with an explicit checkpoint

- Before executing a large chunk of work, write a short plan: what's being built, why, what's already usable vs. genuinely new, sequencing, and what's deliberately out of scope.
- End planning documents with a concrete decision point — the specific things that need confirming before proceeding — rather than proceeding on assumptions.

## 6. Version and narrate the build

- Number deliverable packages/revisions (v1, v2, v3...) rather than silently overwriting.
- Write commit messages that describe the substance of the round of work, not just "update."

## 7. House style, held consistent

- Pick a visual/formatting system once (palette, structure, footer/byline convention) and hold it constant across every document in a project.

## 8. AI's role in the output

- AI is the tool; the person is the architect and is who's named as author. Judgment, verification, and final decisions are the person's — that's stated plainly if ever asked, not hidden and not oversold.
