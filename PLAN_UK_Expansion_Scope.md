## 1. What a real UK market-entry / 50-50 JV plan needs — reasoned from first principles

A startup/expansion plan for this situation has to answer five separate questions, and conflating them is the main way this kind of document ends up shallow:

1. **Is the opportunity real and how big** — market sizing, growth trajectory, why now (regulatory tailwinds: Boiler Upgrade Scheme, 0% VAT on solar, MCS mandate).
2. **Why would a UK installer buy this over Payaca/Reonic/Commusoft/Tradify** — actual differentiated positioning, not assumed superiority. This requires knowing what SalesOps CRM actually does (from the demo) and where it wins/loses against named competitors feature-by-feature.
3. **How do you physically acquire the first 20, then 200 customers** — channel-by-channel: MCS installer database outreach, RECC/HIES association partnerships, trade shows (e.g. Futurebuild, Solar & Storage Live), installer Facebook/WhatsApp communities, content/SEO, referral from RRUP's existing EU customer base, direct sales.
4. **Does the unit economics work** — pricing (need real RRUP pricing data), CAC by channel, gross margin, breakeven customer count, what capital the UK arm needs before it self-funds.
5. **What does the partnership itself need to survive** — 50/50 structures are notorious for deadlock; this needs a decision-rights/deadlock-break mechanism, not just an equity split. This is a legal/commercial-structuring question — I can lay out the standard options and risks, but the actual shareholder agreement needs a solicitor, and I'll flag that explicitly rather than draft legal terms as if they were settled.
