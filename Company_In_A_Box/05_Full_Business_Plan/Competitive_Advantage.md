<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
The advantage is a <b>combination</b>, not a single unassailable feature: proven platform + per-seat price + cross-vertical breadth + founder-led onboarding, aimed at a segment both incumbents underserve. Any one is copyable; the combination, executed with focus, is defensible for the beachhead.</div>

## The four advantages

| Advantage | Durability |
|---|---|
| Proven platform (600+ deployments) | High — cannot be faked by a new competitor |
| Per-seat price wedge | Medium — a competitor could match, but it's against their model |
| Cross-vertical breadth | Medium–High — hard for single-vertical rivals to add quickly |
| Founder-led onboarding | Medium — a service advantage that must be sustained as you scale |

## The honest limit

<div class="warn"><span class="lbl">No single silver bullet</span><br>
None of these alone is an unbreachable moat. The defensibility comes from executing all four, in a focused segment, faster and more attentively than a larger competitor bothers to. That is a real but <b>execution-dependent</b> advantage — it must be earned continuously, not assumed. Say this plainly to the CEO; it is more credible than claiming an imaginary moat.</div>

## How the advantage compounds

Early wins → UK references → easier next wins → density in a region → trade-show credibility → lower CAC. The moat is partly the flywheel of local reference density, which a distant competitor cannot easily replicate.
