<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
Two questions protect everything you build: <b>do you have real UK territorial exclusivity</b>, and <b>who owns the UK customer relationships, data and brand</b> if the partnership ends? Get both answered and documented before investing time and capital — an undocumented answer is the classic way a market-builder gets displaced after doing the hard work.</div>

## 1. Exclusivity ❓

<div class="callout"><span class="lbl">Why it matters</span><br>
If you build the UK market and RRUP can appoint another partner or sell direct into your territory, your effort funds a market you don't control. Confirm: is UK exclusivity granted, for how long, and what conditions (e.g. performance minimums) sustain or revoke it? (Pack 22, Q8.)</div>

## 2. IP & customer ownership

| Asset | Question to resolve |
|---|---|
| The software/IP | Licensed to the UK operation, or owned by RRUP with you as agent? |
| UK customer contracts | Who holds them if the partnership ends? |
| Customer data | Who controls it; how is it handled under UK GDPR (Pack 15)? |
| SalesOps CRM UK brand | Who owns the UK brand and web presence? |

## 3. The protective principle

Value you create (customer relationships, market presence, UK brand equity) should be protected so that a partnership breakdown doesn't strip it from you. This is exactly what a well-drafted agreement secures — and exactly what a verbal understanding fails to.

## Implication

These points feed the terms checklist and the solicitor brief. They are not adversarial toward RRUP — a good partner will want clarity here too; ambiguity serves no one and breeds the kind of dispute the reset is meant to avoid.
