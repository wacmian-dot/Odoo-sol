This brief lets a UK corporate solicitor get to advice fast. It states the situation, the open structural question, and the specific instruments needed — so you pay for judgement, not for the solicitor reconstructing context.</div>

## Brief to the solicitor

**Situation.** UK entrepreneur partnering with a Polish software company (RRUP sp. z o.o.) to launch its product ("SalesOps CRM") in the UK from zero. A prior sales partner misrepresented terms; the parties are restarting cleanly and want everything documented.

**Open question.** The commercial structure is not yet fixed between (a) an equity joint venture (co-owned UK Ltd) and (b) a commission/agency arrangement. Advise on the implications of each for the UK party (tax, liability, control, exit), then paper the chosen one.

**Instruments likely needed:**
| If equity JV | If agency |
|---|---|
| UK Ltd incorporation; Shareholders' Agreement with reserved matters, deadlock, exit; IP licence from RRUP | Agency/reseller agreement with territory exclusivity, commission schedule, clawback limits, liability carve-out, termination tail |

**Cross-cutting:** UK territorial exclusivity; ownership of UK customer data/contracts/brand; UK GDPR / ICO obligations and a Data Processing Agreement with RRUP's EU servers (Pack 15).

<div class="callout"><span class="lbl">What to bring</span><br>
Bring this brief plus the Terms Checklist and the CEO's answers to Pack 22 (Q1, Q3, Q7, Q8, Q9). That package lets the solicitor advise in one efficient session rather than several.</div>

## Implication

Engaging a solicitor with this brief — after the CEO conversation, before signing — is the single most important risk-control step in the whole venture. It is not the place to economise.
