<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
A bottom-up sizing from the verified installer base suggests a UK <b>serviceable market on the order of low-tens of £millions</b> in annual software spend, of which a focused entrant might realistically capture a low-single-digit-percent obtainable share in the first few years. This is a viable niche, not a unicorn market — the numbers below are transparent and every non-verified input is flagged.</div>

## 1. Method

Bottom-up sizing: installer count × software adoption × average annual contract value. This is more honest than a top-down "% of a big market" claim because every driver is inspectable.

## 2. The sizing (illustrative — assumptions flagged) ⚠️

| Layer | Definition | Basis | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TAM** — Total Addressable | All UK MCS-certified installers that could run this software | 5,250+ firms ✅ × est. avg £3,000–6,000/yr contract ⚠️ | ~£16m–£32m/yr ⚠️ |
| **SAM** — Serviceable Available | Solar + heat-pump installers of the target size (Pack 06) | ~40–50% of TAM firms ⚠️ | ~£7m–£15m/yr ⚠️ |
| **SOM** — Serviceable Obtainable (Yr 1–3) | Realistic capture as a new, focused entrant | low-single-digit % of SAM ⚠️ | ~£150k–£600k/yr by Yr 3 ⚠️ |

<div class="warn"><span class="lbl">Read this honestly</span><br>
The <b>installer count is verified</b>; the <b>contract-value and capture assumptions are estimates</b> pending UK pricing (Pack 22) and real pilot conversion data (Pack 08). The ranges are deliberately wide. Treat this as a <b>framework that sharpens the moment UK pricing and early conversion are known</b>, not a forecast. The SOM range is reconciled against the bottom-up revenue model in Pack 16.</div>

## 3. What the sizing tells us

- The prize is **real but bounded** — a focused niche worth low-tens of £millions at the serviceable layer.
- It rewards **efficient, low-CAC entry** (Pack 08) far more than heavy spend — the obtainable share is small in year one by nature.
- It argues for a **lean cost base** (Pack 13) so the venture is viable at modest revenue, and against over-hiring ahead of proven traction.

## 4. Implication

The sizing supports proceeding — with discipline. It does not support a high-burn, land-grab posture. Every downstream plan (staffing, funding, launch pace) should be sized to a niche worth single-digit £millions obtainable, not a mass market.
