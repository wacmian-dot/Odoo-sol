<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
Retention and expansion are where SaaS economics compound (Pack 16). A simple renewal SOP — check health, add value, grow seats — protects the recurring base and turns satisfied customers into referrals (Pack 07) and bigger accounts.</div>

## Procedure

1. **Health check** (quarterly) — are they actively using it? any friction? (early churn warning).
2. **Value review** — remind them of outcomes (time saved, deals won); surface unused features that would help.
3. **Expansion** — as their team grows, add seats (per-seat model rewards this naturally, Pack 02).
4. **Referral ask** — a happy customer at renewal is the best referral source (Pack 07).
5. **Renewal** — confirm continuation ahead of term end; resolve any issues early.

<div class="callout"><span class="lbl">Retention is cheaper than acquisition</span><br>
Keeping a customer costs a fraction of winning one, and a retained customer's seats grow over time. The per-seat model means <b>account growth is automatic revenue growth</b> — nurturing the existing base is often higher-ROI than chasing new logos.</div>

## Implication

This SOP protects the compounding that makes Years 2–3 work (Pack 16) and feeds the referral engine (Pack 07). Churn is the model's enemy; this is the defence.
