<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
This is the most important SOP in the package. Most CRM churn happens in the first 30 days; a structured, hands-on onboarding is what converts a trial into a paying customer. Get the team <b>live and using it by day 7</b> — adoption, not features, is what converts.</div>

## The 30-day procedure

| Phase | Days | Actions |
|---|---|---|
| **Data ingestion** | 1–7 | Migrate historic customers/jobs off spreadsheets; get at least one real workflow running; team logs in |
| **Configuration** | 8–21 | Set up their pricing catalogs, CPQ rules, UK compliance fields (MCS/DNO/ECO4); tailor to their trade |
| **Adoption** | 22–30 | Live training for office + field; verify mobile/photo sync; confirm daily use; pre-close conversation |

<div class="callout"><span class="lbl">Day 7 is the make-or-break</span><br>
If the customer's team isn't logging in and doing real work by day 7, the trial is already drifting toward "no." Front-load the effort: migrate their data and get one genuine workflow live in week one, so by week two they're already dependent on it.</div>

## Ownership

Owned by the Implementation lead (the "personal tutor" role, Pack 13). This role is not overhead — it is the differentiated service that wins the deal (Pack 05) and the single biggest lever on the financial model (Pack 16 sensitivity).

## Implication

Resource this properly. A great product with weak onboarding converts poorly; a good product with excellent onboarding retains. This SOP is where retention is won.
