<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
Segments are tiered by how well they fit the three advantages. Tier 1 (scaling solar/heat-pump installers) is the beachhead; Tier 2 (multi-trade and adjacent trades) is the near expansion; Tier 3 (larger firms with ERP) is the "system of engagement" play once proven. Sequence strictly — do not skip ahead.</div>

## The tiered segment map

| Tier | Segment | Fit | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Beachhead** | MCS solar/heat-pump installers, 10–50 staff | Highest (all 3 advantages) | Now |
| **2a** | Multi-trade "combo" installers (solar + HP + battery) | High (breadth advantage) | After first references |
| **2b** | Adjacent trades — HVAC, construction | Medium (reachable via trade shows) | After first references |
| **3** | Larger installers (£5–50m) already on ERP (SAP/Xero/Sage) | Medium (system-of-engagement play) | Once playbook proven |

## The logic of the sequence

<div class="callout"><span class="lbl">Density before breadth</span><br>
Winning a <b>cluster</b> of Tier 1 installers in one region creates referenceable density — peers talk, case studies compound, trade-show conversations warm up. Jumping to Tier 3 enterprise deals too early spends the lean pod's scarce time on long, complex cycles before the playbook is proven. Earn the right to expand by winning the beachhead first.</div>

## Expansion triggers (evidence-gated)

- Move to **Tier 2** when: 3+ referenceable Tier 1 customers + a repeatable demo-to-paid motion (Pack 10).
- Move to **Tier 3** when: the SAP system-of-engagement pitch (Pack 09) is demo-proven and at least one mid-size reference exists.

## Implication

Segmentation is not a static picture — it is a sequenced plan with explicit triggers. The staffing (Pack 13) and launch (Pack 21) plans assume this order; changing it changes the burn and the risk.
