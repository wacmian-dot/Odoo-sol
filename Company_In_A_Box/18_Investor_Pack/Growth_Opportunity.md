<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
The upside comes from three compounding expansions: <b>more verticals</b> (8 available, 2 used at launch), <b>more segments</b> (small → mid → enterprise via the SAP play), and <b>more geography</b> (region → national). Each is a lever already inside the platform — growth is expansion of a proven base, not a new bet.</div>

## The three growth vectors

| Vector | From → To | Enabler |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical | Solar/HP → all 8 trades | Platform breadth (Pack 02) |
| Segment | Small installers → mid → enterprise | SAP co-existence play (Pack 09) |
| Geography | North West → national | Reference density flywheel (Pack 05) |

<div class="callout"><span class="lbl">Why the upside is credible</span><br>
Each vector uses capability the platform <i>already has</i> — more verticals are already built, the enterprise play uses the existing API, national reach is the same motion repeated. The upside is not "if we build X"; it's "if the proven motion works, here are the adjacent markets it unlocks." That's a lower-risk growth story than most early-stage plans offer.</div>

## The honest ceiling

The obtainable market is a bounded niche (Pack 03 SOM) — this is a strong small-to-mid business opportunity, not a billion-pound outcome. Framing it honestly as such is more credible than inflating it, and matches the capital-efficient, loan-first funding posture.

## Implication

The growth story gives a backer a real reason to expect the business to compound — while the honest ceiling keeps expectations, and valuations, grounded.
