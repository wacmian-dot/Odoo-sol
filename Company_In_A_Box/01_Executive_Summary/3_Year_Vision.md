<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
By Year 3, under base assumptions: a self-funding UK operation with a few hundred retained installer customers, a small regional team, a proven playbook, and the optionality to pursue larger deals (the SAP play) and national reach. A genuinely valuable small-to-mid business, built capital-efficiently.</div>

## The three-year arc

| Year | State | From |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Prove** | Beachhead cluster; ~30 customers (base); lean, loan-funded | Packs 05/16 |
| **2 — Expand** | Adjacent segments/regions; first hires; ~90–120 customers | Packs 06/16 |
| **3 — Scale** | Self-funding; ~200–300 customers; Tier-3 optionality; ~£350k+ your-share revenue | Packs 09/16 |

<div class="callout"><span class="lbl">The honest destination</span><br>
This is a strong small-to-mid business outcome, not a unicorn — and framed that way deliberately. In an equity structure it's real enterprise value; in an agency structure it's substantial recurring income. Which you're building toward is the deal-structure question (Pack 14). Either way, the arc is coherent and the base case reaches a place worth the effort.</div>

## Implication
The three-year vision is the north star for the launch and growth plans (Pack 21/05). All figures are illustrative (Pack 16) until pilot data and the CEO's answers firm them up.
