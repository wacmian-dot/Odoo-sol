## 1. First beachhead segment ⚠️

Start narrower than "all UK installers." Recommended first segment: **MCS-certified solar PV and/or heat pump installers with 10–50 employees** — past the "WhatsApp and a spreadsheet" stage but too small to have already signed a multi-year Payaca/Commusoft contract with switching costs.

This matches RRUP's own pricing curve (the per-seat discounts kick in meaningfully at 11+ and 21+ users — see Folder 01) and is the fastest-growing part of the installer base per the MCS data (new entrants riding the 2026 +50% growth wave).

## 2. Why this segment specifically

- **Large enough to feel the pain.** At 10+ staff, WhatsApp coordination and spreadsheet quoting genuinely start breaking down — lost leads, pricing errors, missed compliance paperwork.
- **Small enough to move fast.** No multi-month corporate procurement cycle, no incumbent enterprise contract to unwind, no committee sign-off.
- **Directly reachable.** Every business in this segment is, by definition, in the public MCS Installation Database (see Folder 05) — the ICP and the lead source are the same list.
- **Growing fastest.** New entrants into the MCS-certified base are disproportionately smaller/newer firms riding the current installation boom — this segment isn't just accessible, it's expanding.

## 3. Secondary segments to consider once the beachhead is proven

- **Construction/HVAC firms** in the same revenue band, reachable via Futurebuild and InstallerSHOW (see Folder 05) rather than the MCS register specifically.
- **Multi-vertical installers** (solar + heat pump + battery combined) — RRUP's cross-vertical breadth (Folder 03) is a specific advantage here that single-vertical competitors like a solar-only tool can't match.
- **Larger Tier-2/3 firms (£5M–£50M revenue)** with existing ERP (SAP, Xero, Sage) — reachable once the "system of engagement" pitch (Folder 06) is proven on smaller accounts first; these deals take longer and carry more integration risk, so they're a second-wave target, not a month-one one.

## 4. Who to deliberately not target yet

- **Sub-5-employee owner-operators** — too price-sensitive and informal to need a structured CRM yet; low ARR, high support burden relative to deal size.
- **Large enterprise accounts already committed to Salesforce/SAP FSL** — long sales cycles, heavy customisation asks, and not where a zero-base UK launch should spend its first six months.
