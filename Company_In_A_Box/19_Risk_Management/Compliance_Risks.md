<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
The main compliance risk is <b>UK GDPR</b>: handling installer and their customers' data, and the cross-border data flow to RRUP's EU servers, without proper registration and agreements. It is low-likelihood if addressed at formation, high-impact if ignored. Fix it on day one (Pack 15).</div>

## Detailed compliance risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Operating without ICO registration | Regulatory exposure | Register with the ICO at company formation (Pack 15) |
| No Data Processing Agreement with RRUP (EU servers) | Unlawful cross-border processing | Put a DPA in place as part of the partnership (Pack 14/15) |
| Non-compliant outbound (cold email) | ICO complaint, reputational | Lawful basis + opt-out + suppression from the first email (Pack 08/15) |
| Handling homeowner data (via installers) | Data-subject risk | Clear controller/processor roles; data-handling policy (Pack 15) |
| Using a prior employer's data | Legal breach | Never import third-party proprietary lists (Pack 06/08) |

<div class="warn"><span class="lbl">Non-negotiable from day one</span><br>
UK GDPR compliance is not a "later" item. ICO registration and a DPA are cheap and fast to put in place, and their absence turns a routine operation into a liability. Handle at formation.</div>

## Implication

Compliance risk is fully controllable and inexpensive to mitigate — provided it's done at the start. Pack 15 provides the actual policies; this register ensures it's on the day-one checklist (Pack 21).
