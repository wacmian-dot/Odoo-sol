<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
The operational risks centre on <b>dependence on RRUP</b> (product, localisation, demo environment) and <b>onboarding capacity</b>. They're mitigated by confirming RRUP's obligations in writing (Pack 14) and by treating onboarding quality as a core, resourced function (Pack 12/13).</div>

## Detailed operational risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Demo/trial instance down (happened once) | Failed pitch | Confirm environment live before every demo; keep a UAT screenshot fallback (Pack 09) |
| RRUP localisation gap (MCS/VAT not built) | Deals stall mid-cycle | Confirm scope with RRUP (Q7); demo-verify (Pack 20); scope honestly with prospects |
| Onboarding under-resourced → trials fizzle | Low trial→paid | Dedicated implementation lead; structured 30-day process (Pack 12) |
| Over-reliance on RRUP support responsiveness | Delivery delays | Agree SLAs/obligations in the partnership terms (Pack 14) |
| Founder-dependency (you are the operation early) | Single point of failure | Document SOPs (Pack 12); hire the pod as traction allows (Pack 13) |

<div class="callout"><span class="lbl">The dependency to manage</span><br>
The UK operation depends on RRUP for the product, its reliability, and much of its localisation. That dependency is normal for this model but must be <b>made explicit and contractual</b> (Pack 14), not assumed — so that RRUP's obligations to you are as documented as yours to the customer.</div>

## Implication

Operational risk is manageable but hinges on a well-defined RRUP relationship (Pack 14) and a properly resourced onboarding function (Pack 12) — both already built into the plan.
