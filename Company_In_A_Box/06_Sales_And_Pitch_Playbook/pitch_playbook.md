## 1. The core pitch motion ⚠️

1. **Lead with the free 30-day trial** — RRUP already offers this in Poland; carrying it to the UK removes the biggest first-call objection ("prove it works before I switch off my spreadsheet").
2. **Open with the vertical-specific angle, not "we're a CRM."** "Built specifically for solar and heat pump installers, not adapted from a generic trade tool" is a sharper hook than competing head-on with Payaca on features you haven't verified you match.
3. **Use the MCS-compliance and commission-automation hooks** as the two concrete pains to lead with — paperwork/compliance burden and manual commission calculation are named, specific frustrations for growing installer businesses, and both map directly to features RRUP already has.
4. **Reference-sell as soon as you have 2–3 UK logos.** The Polish testimonials prove the product works, but a UK installer will trust a UK peer's name far more — get your first few customers to agree to a case study early and use it aggressively.
5. **Trade-show presence + MCS/RECC channel partnership** does the "who do I even call" problem for you at scale, while cold outreach against the MCS database does the immediate pipeline-filling in month one.

## 2. The "leaky bucket" framing ⚠️ (pattern worth using, wording is illustrative)

Move away from generic feature selling. Anchor outreach on quantified operational pain rather than the product itself — for example:

> "We work with UK renewable installers whose field reps lose deals because estimates take too long to turn around, and whose margins get eaten by paperwork and manual commission calculation. We've built a system that compiles a compliant quotation and secures a signature on-site in minutes, not days."

**Any specific numbers used in a live pitch (e.g. "15% of deals," "under 7 minutes") should be sourced from your own early customer conversations, not invented** — these are illustrative starting points for the *shape* of the pitch, not verified statistics to print in a deck.

## 3. Enterprise/mid-market objection handling ✅ (the SAP positioning — genuinely solid)

For any Tier-2/3 contractor already running SAP, Xero, Sage, or QuickBooks: don't position SalesOps CRM as a replacement. Position it as an agile **system of engagement** sitting on top of their existing **system of record**, handling vertical-specific calculations (solar array layout, heat pump flow/COP, BTU sizing) via clean API sync — work that would otherwise cost tens of thousands in custom development to bolt onto a generic ERP. This directly defuses the "why would I add another system" objection from a finance-literate buyer.

## 4. What to never do in a pitch until confirmed

- **Do not reference Grupa Polsat Plus or any other client not directly confirmed by RRUP** — see Folder 01 and Folder 13, Question 6. An unverifiable client claim is a real reputational and credibility risk if a prospect checks it.
- **Do not quote specific competitive feature gaps against Payaca or Reonic** you haven't personally verified against the live demo — see Folder 03. Positioning theses are fine to hold privately; specific "we do X and they don't" claims need direct verification first.
- **Do not quote UK pricing** until it's actually been set with RRUP — the Polish price list in Folder 01 is a reference point, not a UK quote.

## 5. Early reference-building priority

Getting your first 2–3 UK customers to agree to a named case study is disproportionately valuable — it converts every claim above from "trust me" to "here's a UK company like yours who'll tell you directly." Prioritise this over volume in the first quarter.
