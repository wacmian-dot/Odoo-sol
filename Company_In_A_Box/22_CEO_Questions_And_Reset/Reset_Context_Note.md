<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
Approach the reset as a positive, professional fresh start, not a post-mortem. The CEO already identified the problem and chose to deal with you directly — that's a vote of confidence. Lead with transparency and preparation; the ten questions demonstrate you take the relationship seriously.</div>

## The framing

- **Acknowledge the reset briefly, then look forward.** The CEO knows what happened; dwelling on Bart adds nothing. A single line ("I'm glad we're dealing directly — let's build this on a clear footing") is enough.
- **Lead with preparation.** This whole package signals you've done serious, honest work. That is the trust-builder — far more than any reassurance.
- **Be transparent about what you don't know.** Presenting the ten questions (and this package's honest flags) shows integrity — the opposite of Bart's approach, and exactly what rebuilds trust.

<div class="callout"><span class="lbl">Transparency is the strategy</span><br>
The Bart episode was about misrepresentation. Your entire differentiation as a partner is the opposite: verified facts, flagged unknowns, nothing overclaimed. Handing the CEO a package that openly says "here's what I've confirmed, here's what I need you to confirm" is the single most powerful trust signal available — and it's true.</div>

## What to bring to the conversation

The ten questions (this Pack), and a willingness to share the relevant parts of this package. You don't need to reveal every internal working note, but the transparency of the approach should be evident.

## Implication

Handled well, the reset becomes a competitive advantage: a CEO burned by a dishonest partner meets a prepared, transparent one. That contrast does more for the relationship than any pitch.
