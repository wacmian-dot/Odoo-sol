<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
The demo is not a feature tour — it is a <b>tailored re-enactment of the prospect's own workflow</b>, ending on the two differentiators (Work-Flow automation, cross-vertical breadth) and a trial sign-up. Show their pain solved, not the product's menu.</div>

## The 20-minute structure

| Minutes | Segment | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Recap their pain | "You told me quotes take X and commission maths eats Y hours…" |
| 3–10 | Their workflow, live | Lead → branded quote → e-signed contract → job → auto commission |
| 10–15 | The two differentiators | Work-Flow automation; one system across their trades |
| 15–18 | Proof + price | 600+ deployments; per-seat priced to start small |
| 18–20 | Trial close | "Let's get your data in and your team using it free for 30 days." |

## Tailoring rules

<div class="callout"><span class="lbl">Make it theirs</span><br>
Where possible, configure the demo environment with the prospect's trade, product types and branding so it looks built for them. A solar installer should see solar; a heat-pump installer should see heat-pump. Generic demos convert far worse than ones where the prospect sees their own business on screen.</div>

## Discipline / boundaries

<div class="warn"><span class="lbl">Do not over-claim</span><br>
Only demonstrate features you can actually show working. UK-specific depth (MCS/DNO/ECO4 automation, offline field sync) must be <b>demo-verified before it is shown or promised</b> (Pack 20). Never reference the Polsat claim. Never pitch on a demo instance you haven't confirmed is live that morning — the environment was down once already.</div>

## Implication

A repeatable, tailored demo is the highest-conversion asset the pod owns. Building 2–3 pre-configured vertical demo environments (solar, heat pump, multi-trade) is a worthwhile early investment for the Implementation lead (Pack 12/13).
