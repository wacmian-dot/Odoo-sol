<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
Discovery does two jobs at once: <b>qualify</b> (is this a winnable ICP deal?) and <b>quantify the pain in pounds</b> (the basis of the whole business case). Use a light MEDDIC spine so a lean team qualifies ruthlessly and never demos into a dead end.</div>

## The discovery spine (MEDDIC-informed)

| Element | Question to answer |
|---|---|
| **Metrics** | What is the status quo costing? (quote turnaround, deals slipped, admin hours, commission errors) |
| **Economic buyer** | Is the MD/owner engaged or reachable? (Pack 06 personas) |
| **Decision criteria** | What must be true for them to switch? (price, adoption ease, UK compliance) |
| **Decision process** | Who signs, what's the timeline, what's the trigger? |
| **Identify pain** | The 2–3 concrete, daily frustrations — costed |
| **Champion** | Usually the ops lead — who feels the pain most? |

## The pain-quantification move

<div class="callout"><span class="lbl">Turn pain into pounds</span><br>
"How long does a quote take today, and how many do you lose to slow turnaround?" → an estimated £/month of leaked revenue. "How many hours a week on compliance paperwork and commission maths?" → an hourly cost. These become the prospect's own numbers in the business case — far more persuasive than any feature list. Use the prospect's figures, never invented ones.</div>

## Qualification gates

Advance to a demo only when: ICP-fit confirmed (Pack 06), at least two pains quantified, and the economic buyer is engaged or a credible champion exists. Otherwise, nurture — don't burn the lean pod's demo time.

## Implication

Good discovery is what lets a small team punch above its weight: fewer, better-qualified demos convert far more than many unqualified ones. This framework feeds the demo (next) and the pipeline exit criteria (Pack 10).
