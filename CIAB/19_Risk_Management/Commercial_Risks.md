<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
The commercial risks are competition, slow adoption, and CAC uncertainty. Each is mitigated by the same disciplines already built into the plan: a focused segment, a free-trial motion, and CAC-gated spend. The plan is designed to fail cheaply and learn fast, not to bet big on unproven assumptions.</div>

## Detailed commercial risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Payaca/Reonic out-compete or out-spend | Lost deals, higher CAC | Compete on focus + price wedge + speed, not spend (Pack 04); own the small-installer segment they underserve |
| Slow adoption (installers stay on spreadsheets) | Long sales cycles | Lead with cost-of-status-quo pitch; free trial removes risk (Pack 09) |
| CAC higher than assumed | Model breaks | CAC-gated paid spend (Pack 07); free MCS channel keeps blended CAC low (Pack 08) |
| UK pricing set too high (kills wedge) | Lost differentiation | Model the trade-off explicitly (Pack 16); pricing is an early CEO agenda item |
| First references slow to materialise | Credibility gap persists | Prioritise first 2–3 UK case studies on preferential terms (Pack 09) |

<div class="callout"><span class="lbl">The structural mitigation</span><br>
Most commercial risk is contained by one design choice: <b>a lean, low-burn operation that starts with a free lead channel and gates spend on proven conversion.</b> This means the downside of being wrong about the market is small — you learn cheaply rather than burning capital on an unproven thesis.</div>

## Implication

Commercial risk is real but bounded by design. The single biggest lever to reduce it further is getting the first UK references fast — which is why it recurs as a priority across Packs 05, 07 and 09.
