<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
The partnership itself carries the venture's most consequential risks: <b>structural ambiguity, exclusivity that isn't real, deadlock, and mismatched expectations</b>. The Bart episode already showed how misaligned understanding causes damage. The mitigation is uniform: document everything, verify claims, and align expectations explicitly with the CEO now.</div>

## Detailed partnership risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Deal structure undocumented/ambiguous | Everything downstream unstable | Resolve + document (Pack 14, Q1) |
| Exclusivity not genuinely granted | You build a market you don't control | Confirm in writing (Q8; Pack 14) |
| 50/50 deadlock (if equity) | Business paralysis | Deadlock clause (Pack 14) |
| Expectation mismatch on timeline/funding | Relationship breakdown | Align explicitly (Q10); realistic ramp (Pack 05) |
| Repeat of misrepresentation | Wasted effort, mistrust | Verify claims independently; nothing verbal |
| Over-reliance on an unverified asset (Polsat) | Credibility damage | Do not use until confirmed (Pack 02, Q6) |

<div class="callout"><span class="lbl">The lesson already learned</span><br>
The Bart situation is not just a past problem — it's a <b>risk model</b>. It shows exactly how this venture gets hurt: through unverified claims and undocumented understandings. The entire discipline of this package — verify, document, flag — is the direct mitigation. Carry it into the CEO relationship deliberately.</div>

## Implication

Partnership risk is best mitigated in the reset conversation itself (Pack 22) and the resulting written agreement (Pack 14). Getting these right is worth more than any amount of downstream planning.
