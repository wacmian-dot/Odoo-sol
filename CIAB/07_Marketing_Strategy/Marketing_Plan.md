<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
Marketing is <b>outbound-and-credibility-led, not brand-spend-led</b>. The budget goes to the two things that move a niche B2B market: showing up where installers gather (trade shows, trade bodies) and building undeniable proof (UK case studies). Broad paid brand advertising is the wrong tool for a 5,250-firm market.</div>

## 1. Priorities (in order)

| Priority | Activity | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Direct outbound (Pack 08) | Cheapest, most-qualified demand in a small named market |
| 2 | Trade-show presence | Where the entire ICP physically gathers |
| 3 | Reference/case-study engine | Proof is the currency in a trust-driven trade |
| 4 | Content/SEO for intent terms | Capture the installers actively searching |
| 5 | Trade-body co-marketing (RECC/HIES) | Borrowed credibility at scale |
| 6 | Targeted paid (validate CAC) | Only once message + funnel are proven |

## 2. Why not big brand spend

<div class="callout"><span class="lbl">Right tool, right market</span><br>
With ~5,250 target firms, you can practically <b>name every prospect</b> (Pack 06). In that world, a £-for-£ pound spent on precise outbound and trade-show presence beats broad advertising many times over. Marketing's job here is to make the named list warm and to manufacture proof — not to build mass awareness.</div>

## 3. Budget logic ⚠️

Marketing spend should be modest and traction-gated (Pack 05 growth phases): a trade-show stand or two, basic web/content, minimal paid until CAC is proven. Exact figures sit in the financial model's Assumptions Register (Pack 16) as validated inputs, not guesses.

## Implication

The marketing plan is deliberately lean and precise — a fit for both the small market and the loan-funded budget. It hands most of the demand-generation load to outbound (Pack 08) and proof (case studies), which is where a focused newcomer wins.
