<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
Prior material described <b>two incompatible deals</b> — an equity joint venture and a commission/agency arrangement. They differ fundamentally on capital, liability, control and upside. This document does not assume which is real; it compares them so the CEO conversation (Pack 22, Q1) resolves it deliberately. Everything financial and legal downstream depends on this answer.</div>

## The two structures, compared

| Dimension | Equity JV (co-owned UK Ltd) | Commission / Agency |
|---|---|---|
| Your stake | Ownership share (e.g. 50/50) | No equity; % of contract value |
| Capital at risk | Yes — you fund/own part of an operating company | Low — you fund your own sales activity |
| Liability | Shared operating liability | Typically none for product/engineering (to confirm) |
| Upside | Enterprise value if it grows and is sold | Ongoing commission income |
| Control | Board rights, reserved matters | Contractual, not ownership control |
| Downside | Real — jointly liable for a company | Limited — lose time/effort, not capital |
| Funding relevance | Start Up Loan / equity injection | Mainly self-funded sales overheads |

## Why this must be resolved first

<div class="warn"><span class="lbl">Everything hangs on this</span><br>
The legal form (Pack 14), the funding plan (Pack 16/17), the financial model's revenue treatment (Pack 16), and your personal risk all change completely between these two structures. Proceeding without resolving it would mean building half the package on sand. It is Question 1 to the CEO for exactly this reason.</div>

## A decision lens (not a recommendation)

Neither is "better" in the abstract — it depends on your appetite:
- **Equity JV** suits you if you want ownership upside and are willing to carry capital and operating risk.
- **Agency** suits you if you want lower risk and faster income with less capital exposure, accepting no equity.

The right choice is a conversation about what each side actually contributes and wants (Pack 22, Q1/Q3/Q9) — informed by a solicitor before signing.
