<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
RRUP has genuine, named, mid-market client testimonials that are safe to reference. It does <b>not</b> — on any evidence found — have a verifiable "Grupa Polsat Plus" flagship. Using an unverifiable client claim in a UK pitch is a real credibility and legal risk. Use the real references; confirm or drop the Polsat claim with the CEO first.</div>

These are real, on-record, and consistent with a proven mid-market vendor. They are usable in UK conversations today — with the honest framing that they are Polish references (a UK peer reference is more persuasive, which is why building your first UK case studies fast is a priority — Pack 09).

## 2. The Polsat claim — handle with discipline 🚩

<div class="warn"><span class="lbl">Do not use until confirmed in writing</span><br>
Prior planning material asserted <b>Grupa Polsat Plus</b> — a multi-billion-euro Polish media/energy conglomerate — as RRUP's flagship "anchor tenant" and built objection-handling scripts around it. Independent checking found <b>no evidence</b>: it is absent from RRUP's own website (which does name its real, smaller clients), and no public source connects the two. This has the signature of a fabricated credibility claim from a prior AI tool.<br><br>
<b>Why it matters more than a normal error:</b> stated to the CEO and false, it signals you don't understand their business; stated to a UK prospect who checks it, it is a false third-party claim made in a commercial pitch. Both are avoidable. Ask the CEO directly (Pack 22, Q6). If real, obtain the case detail and use it properly. If not, drop it entirely.</div>

## 3. Implication

Credibility in the UK pitch should rest on three verifiable pillars — **the platform's proven scale** (600+ deployments), **the real named references above**, and **the live demo** — none of which depend on Polsat. Treat Polsat as upside to be confirmed, never as a load-bearing claim.
