<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
The investment thesis is clean: back a lean UK operator to localise a <b>proven</b> European platform into a fast-growing, policy-backed market that a VC-funded competitor (Reonic) is already validating — at a fraction of Reonic's capital intensity. The near-term funding need is small (a Start Up Loan may suffice); outside equity is a growth-phase option, not a launch necessity.</div>

## The thesis in five points

1. **Proven product** — 600+ deployments removes product risk (Pack 02).
2. **Real, growing market** — policy-backed, +50% forecast (Pack 03).
3. **Validated by a funded competitor** — Reonic's €13M UK push confirms the space (Pack 04).
4. **Capital-efficient entry** — free lead channel, lean pod; wins on focus not spend (Pack 05/08).
5. **Underserved segment** — small installers both incumbents price past (Pack 04/06).

## Unit economics framing ⚠️

<div class="callout"><span class="lbl">What an investor will test</span><br>
The decisive metrics are <b>LTV/CAC</b> and <b>payback period</b>. With a near-free primary lead channel (MCS database) and recurring per-seat revenue, blended CAC can be low and LTV compounds with retention — a potentially attractive ratio. But these are <b>assumptions to prove with pilot data</b> (Pack 16), not yet demonstrated. An honest investor case says so.</div>

## Why the raise (if any) is modest

The launch is designed to run on a Start Up Loan. Outside equity only makes sense to accelerate Year-2/3 expansion once unit economics are proven — a far stronger position to raise from than pre-traction.

## Implication

Hold this pack for the growth phase and the equity scenario. Its most useful near-term function is disciplining the plan to investor-grade logic (unit economics, capital efficiency) even while self/loan-funded.
