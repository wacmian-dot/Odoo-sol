<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
Reonic is less a head-to-head threat today than a <b>strategic mirror</b>: a German renewables-installer platform, VC-backed (€13M Series A 2024), 3,000+ EU customers, now expanding into the UK — the exact EU-to-UK move this venture is attempting. Its trajectory both validates the thesis and warns that a well-capitalised competitor is moving on the same ground.</div>

## 1. Profile ✅

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Origin | Germany, founded 2021 |
| Funding | €13M Series A (2024); also reported $15.5M from Northzone, Point Nine, Puzzle Ventures |
| Scale | 3,000+ customers across Europe; 2,000+ reported in 2025 |
| Expansion | Berlin office; expanding into France, Italy, UK, LATAM |
| Capability | Lead management, CRM, project design/simulation, procurement, proposals, e-signature, install tracking, billing |
| Pricing | Not public; enterprise/quote (~$25k minimum projects reported) |

## 2. What Reonic validates ✅

<div class="callout"><span class="lbl">The thesis is not eccentric</span><br>
A credible VC syndicate funded a continental renewables-installer platform to expand into the UK. That is independent confirmation that <b>the EU-to-UK renewables-software entry is a real, investable opportunity</b> — the same one SalesOps CRM is pursuing. The pattern-match is reassuring for the business case (Pack 05, Pack 18).</div>

## 3. What Reonic warns

- **Capital asymmetry** — a €13M-funded competitor can outspend a lean, loan-funded entrant on marketing and product. Do not try to win a spending war; win on focus, price model and speed in a specific segment.
- **Enterprise tilt** — Reonic's opaque, higher-end pricing leaves the **small-installer segment relatively open** — the same wedge Payaca's flat pricing leaves (Pack 02, Pack 06). This is the consistent gap in the market: the small, cost-conscious, fast-growing installer.

## 4. Implication

Reonic makes two things clear: the opportunity is real (invest with confidence in the thesis), and the small-installer, price-led segment is the right place to plant the flag — it is where neither well-funded incumbent is strongest.
