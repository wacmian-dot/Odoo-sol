<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
Repayment is funded by <b>recurring subscription revenue</b>, which compounds monthly as customers are retained. Spread over 1–5 years at 7.5% fixed, monthly repayments are modest against even the base-case revenue by Year 1–2 — the recurring model makes repayment coverage comfortable rather than fragile.</div>

## Coverage logic

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Recurring revenue | Predictable monthly income to service the loan |
| 1–5 year term | Spreads repayment; keeps monthly cost low |
| 7.5% fixed | No rate-rise risk over the term |
| No early-repayment fee | Overpay from surplus in good months |

<div class="callout"><span class="lbl">Why recurring revenue de-risks repayment</span><br>
Unlike a project business with lumpy income, a SaaS subscription base produces steady monthly cash that grows as customers accumulate. By the time the base-case customer count is reached (Pack 16), monthly recurring revenue comfortably exceeds the monthly loan repayment — the coverage improves over time rather than degrading.</div>

## Stress view

Even in the bear scenario (Pack 16), the lean cost base and long repayment term keep repayment serviceable, if tighter. The plan is built to repay from the downside case, not only the upside.

## Implication

The repayment story is one of a lender's favourites: recurring revenue, long term, fixed rate, coverage that strengthens over time. It supports requesting toward the upper end of the loan range with confidence.
