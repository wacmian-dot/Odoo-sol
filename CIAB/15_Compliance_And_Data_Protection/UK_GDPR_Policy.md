<div class="bluf"><span class="lbl">Bottom line up front</span><br>
Operating in the UK means handling personal data — installer contacts and, via the CRM, their customers' data. That triggers UK GDPR: <b>ICO registration, a lawful basis for processing, and a Data Processing Agreement with RRUP's EU servers</b>. All are cheap and fast to put in place, and non-negotiable from formation.</div>

## The baseline obligations ✅

| Obligation | Action |
|---|---|
| ICO registration | Register the UK entity with the Information Commissioner's Office |
| Lawful basis | Legitimate interest for B2B outreach (with opt-out); consent where required |
| Data Processing Agreement | With RRUP (data flows to EU servers) — controller/processor roles defined |
| Data subject rights | Process for access/erasure requests |
| Security | Appropriate technical/organisational measures |
| Records | Record of processing activities |

<div class="warn"><span class="lbl">Cross-border flow</span><br>
Because SalesOps CRM's servers are in the EU, UK→EU personal-data transfer must be covered by an appropriate mechanism and a DPA with RRUP (Pack 14). This is a specific, checkable item — confirm the data architecture with RRUP (Pack 20) and paper it with the solicitor.</div>

## Boundary

This is a compliance baseline, not legal advice — the specifics (especially the cross-border mechanism) should be confirmed by the solicitor engaged for the partnership (Pack 14). But the obligations themselves are clear and standard.

## Implication

Handled at formation, compliance is cheap and routine. Ignored, it becomes a liability (Pack 19). It goes on the day-one launch checklist (Pack 21).
